INTUBATALK · ICU CLINICAL SYSTEM

Usage Guidelines

Set-up, pairing, and safe day-to-day use for the patient tablet and the nurse dashboard.

01

Set up the bedside tablet

  1. Open the patient tablet page and enter the patient's name (bed number is optional).
  2. A four-digit room code appears in the top bar. Write it where the care team can see it.
  3. Prop the tablet where the patient can reach and see it, with the screen brightness up.
  4. Open Abilities and mark what the patient can do today — the tiles adapt to that profile.

A fresh session starts every time the tablet page is opened, so re-admission to a new bed always creates a new code.

02

Set up the nurse dashboard

  1. Open the nurse dashboard and sign in with your name — it is attached to every acknowledgement.
  2. Tap Add Patient to connect one or many beds at once: paste several room codes separated by commas, or create new patients by name and give each generated code to its bedside tablet.
  3. Filter the feed by bed or by urgency; emergency alarms interrupt with a full-screen siren until acknowledged.
  4. Use Test to check the chime and the spoken read-aloud during shift handover.
03

Eye-blink protocol

  1. Establish a clear convention aloud first: one blink = yes, two blinks = no.
  2. Ask one question at a time and wait at least five seconds for an answer.
  3. Optionally enable the camera assist on the blink panel, so the nurse can watch the patient's eyes from the tablet screen. The camera is only used if permission is granted, and the video never leaves the device.
  4. Record the answer with the YES / NO buttons — the tablet relays and speaks it to the dashboard.
Never use the blink answer alone for decisions about pain, sedation, or withdrawal of care. Confirm with a full clinical assessment.
04

Emergencies and alarms

  • The red Emergency tile flashes the screen, rings a repeating siren on the dashboard, and vibrates — it repeats until a nurse acknowledges.
  • Suction, breathing, toilet and pain ≥7 are high-priority: they pulse the feed card and are spoken aloud.
  • When the patient recovers, they tap “I am okay now” which cancels the alarm on both sides.
  • If the tablet is offline or muffled, the hand-cued laminated board is the backup — keep it within the patient's sight line.
05

Hygiene, privacy and care limits

  • Wipe the tablet and laminated board with hospital-approved disinfectant wipes between patients.
  • Sign out and clear the session when the bed changes; the dashboard caches the patient list on the nurse device only.
  • INTUBATALK relays requests — it does not replace continuous ECG monitoring, call bells, or a bedside-sitting nurse when clinically needed.
  • Speak with the patient, not about them: the tablet's voice is the patient's voiceBOX, so acknowledge at the bedside.
06

Downtime and the laminated board

If Wi-Fi, the tablet, or the dashboard is unavailable, switch to the printable backup board:

  1. Print the board in A4 landscape from the Backup board page and laminate it.
  2. Point at a tile to communicate; for emergencies, keep pointing until a nurse arrives.
  3. Prop it where the same position is used on the tablet, so the mapping is identical.

Quick reference

One tap = one request. Thirst, reposition, nausea, anxiety send instantly.
Four tiles open a phrase picker: breathing, suction, toilet, and the 0–10 pain scale.
1 blink / 2 blinks = yes / no. Confirm the convention aloud each shift.
Red card = emergency. It repeats until acknowledged.
Add Patient = paste one code, or many, or create patients and hand out codes.
Start over on the tablet wipes the session between beds.
